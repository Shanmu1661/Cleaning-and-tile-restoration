The ASTM D523 Optical Measurement Scale

Gloss Units (GU) quantify the specular reflectance of light directed at a 60° angle. A digital optical sensor calculates the ratio of reflected to incident light.

Dull / Acid-Etched Stone 15 – 22 GU
Diffusely scattered light; zero chandelier reflection.
Commercial Floor Wax Coating 50 – 58 GU
Artificial plastic film; yellows, scratches, and peels within months.

Frequently Asked Questions by Stone Owners

Everything you need to know about diamond honing, acid burn remediation, and stone longevity.

Natural marble, limestone, and travertine are composed of calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$). Any acid (vinegar, lemon juice, wine, perfume, or acidic tile cleaner) chemically eats away the polished surface, creating a dull white chemical burn known as an etch mark ($CaCO_3 + 2CH_3COOH ightarrow Ca(CH_3COO)_2 + H_2O + CO_2$). It is not a surface stain that can be washed off; the top microns of stone have literally dissolved. It must be mechanically diamond-honed flush to restore specular clarity.

Unlike store waxes or polyurethane coats that scratch and yellow within 6 months, our crystallization alters the top layer of stone into hard fluorosilicate. Waxes and topical sealers sit as a plastic coating on top of stone. They trap moisture underneath, turn cloudy yellow from UV rays, and scratch under foot traffic, requiring toxic chemical stripping. Molecular crystallization, conversely, is an electrochemical polish: fine polishing acids and diamond pads react with calcium carbonate to form a dense micro-crystalline mirror surface that breathes naturally and cannot peel.
